# Chapter 10 — Moderation and discipline

> **Status:** Moderation authority intended; disciplinary process open  
> **Source:** Consolidated guild governance framework, 10 November 2026, v0.1. Working material, not a ratified constitution.

Autonomous authority intended · Formal disciplinary policy open

The earlier design permits Eldgrim to issue warnings, demotions, and graduated restrictions, including short, medium, long, and permanent timeouts or removal.

For unclear disputes, the earlier discussion proposed hearing from the involved people once before deciding. Dice or chance were also considered for situations that remain unresolved.

The present design does not yet establish:

- Which conduct warrants each sanction.
- Exact timeout durations.
- Whether a first offense normally receives a warning.
- Evidence standards and treatment of conflicting accounts.
- When urgent intervention may occur outside the daily cycle.
- How members can contest an incorrect decision.
- Who can reverse a technically mistaken or harmful action.
- Whether permanent removal requires additional safeguards.

Randomness can serve roleplay or harmless tie-breaking. It should not determine whether a member is guilty of harassment or deserves permanent exclusion.

## Impartial founder complaints and consistent standards — questions 155–156

When the founder is personally involved in a complaint or appeal, a **designated impartial human reviewer** decides the merits (Q155). The founder may take necessary immediate protective action but cannot use technical ownership to retaliate or obstruct fair review. The reviewer does not thereby gain direct access to Eldgrim's founder-PC-local memory database (Q138); an authorized, privacy-preserving evidence procedure remains to be designed.

Friendship, rank, long service and personal familiarity never alter evidence standards, misconduct definitions, proportionality, hearing or appeal rights (Q156). Harmless storytelling and personal recognition may reflect relationships, but do not establish guilt, innocence or automatic reward entitlement. These rules are **confirmed working policy**, not a deployed appeals process.

## No retroactive discipline and limited jurisdiction — questions 164–165

A new rule cannot justify a sanction for conduct permitted when it occurred (Q164). Present credible safety risks may still require proportionate protective action under rules already in force, with reasons and appeal. This must not be used as disguised retroactive punishment.

Eldgrim's disciplinary scope is guild spaces, guild activities and conduct directly affecting guild safety or membership (Q165), not unrelated private lives, other communities or personal accounts. Reliable evidence of external conduct matters only when it establishes a direct guild concern; existing evidence, hearing, privacy and appeal safeguards remain applicable.

**Status:** Founder-confirmed working policy; not evidence of implemented autonomous moderation.

## Confirmed appeal intake, evidence and remedies — questions 171–180

**Appeal access and timing (Q171–173).** Members may challenge consequential warnings, demotions, timeouts, kicks and bans through a **confidential bot or designated human route**, with a usable fallback after Discord removal. No public accusation or unnecessary personal disclosure is required. There is **no absolute appeal-expiry deadline**; credible new evidence or a significant procedural mistake may justify later review, subject to the availability of evidence and approved retention rules. Where operationally possible, acknowledge an appeal **within 7 calendar days** and aim to decide it **within 14 days**. Communicate delays and never claim that a review occurred when it did not. These are response targets, not automatic decisions.

**Proof and protection (Q174–176).** Consequential sanctions require specific, reasonably reliable evidence connecting the member to the conduct. Consider context, corroboration and conflicting accounts. Rumor, internal standing, uncertain character identity or unverified AI summaries are not independently sufficient. Severe sanctions demand stronger confidence. Examine only authorized case material and preserve the minimum justified case findings, references and audit metadata under Q136. Never create a permanent guild-chat transcript or import incident records into narrative memory. A pending investigation may justify only a proportionate, least-restrictive **temporary protective** measure supported by credible current risk; explain and promptly review it. It is not a finding of guilt.

**Independent review and correction (Q177–178).** A founder-involved complaint goes to a designated impartial human who was not personally involved. Provide necessary evidence through a controlled process without direct access to the founder's PC-local memory database (Q138); the founder must not suppress relevant evidence or direct the outcome. A successful appeal reverses/reduces sanctions, restores feasible roles/permissions, corrects administrative records and informs the member. Any public correction must be proportionate and protect confidential case details.

**Unfounded allegations and unresolved cases (Q179–180).** Protect good-faith reporting, including unproven reports. Deliberate fabrication or retaliation requires separate reliable evidence and ordinary fair process; unsuccessful reports do not imply malice. If guilt remains unresolved, record uncertainty and impose no speculative sanction. Any continuing protection must be based on credible current risk, reviewed periodically and lifted when no longer justified.

**Status:** Founder-confirmed **working governance**, not a ratified charter or live appeal/moderation workflow. Exact intake channel, after-ban access, reviewer appointment, secure case evidence handling and timing automation are **not implemented or verified**. Q136's ordinary **180-day maximum** for administrative audits remains subject to documented exceptions; Q172 does not authorize indefinite case archives.

## Confirmed guardrails — questions 20, 42, 49, 57 and 62

- Serious misconduct or repeated established-rule violations may justify suspension or removal, with proportionality, recorded reasons and appeal.
- An acute safety threat may justify immediate protective removal.
- Demotion requires misconduct or abuse of delegated responsibilities; inactivity never qualifies.
- Negative standing adjustments require verified evidence. Rumors, ambiguous jokes and unsupported model inferences cannot independently produce disciplinary consequences.
- Consequential decisions must be explained to those affected while preserving confidentiality.
- Eldgrim cannot create offenses beyond the existing guild rules.

**Still open:** exact sanctions and durations, evidence thresholds, right of reply, independent appeal reviewer, exceptional emergency procedure and retention/correction of disciplinary records.


## Interview update — questions 63 and 65

- **Q63 — Established:** Standing alone cannot justify sanctions; a specific existing rule violation must be established independently.
- **Q65 — Established severity classes:** **Minor**: isolated discourtesy or disruption. **Moderate**: repeated disrespect or continued disruption after a warning. **Serious**: targeted harassment, discrimination, threats, scams or abuse of entrusted duties. **Critical**: credible immediate safety threats, doxxing or severe targeted abuse. Classification considers intent, context, reliable evidence and actual harm. These classes do not yet set automatic penalties.
- **Q64 — Confirmed:** Internal standing and personalization data are not disclosed through member-facing features, and there is no standing-score appeal process. **Consequential administrative actions remain explainable and appealable** under Q20, Q42 and Q49, without exposing private scores or personal profiles. Lawful privacy rights remain applicable.


## Confirmed disciplinary procedures — questions 66–68

- **Q66 — Graduated response:** Minor: reminder or warning. Moderate: formal warning or short timeout. Serious: longer timeout, demotion or removal when justified. Critical: immediate protective suspension, with permanent removal possible. Context and proportionality matter; severity does not automatically dictate a fixed sanction.
- **Q67 — Timeout durations:** **Short: 1 hour. Medium: 24 hours. Long: 7 days.** Permanent exclusion is a **ban**, not an indefinite timeout. A lesser response may be chosen when appropriate.
- **Q68 — Fair hearing:** Eldgrim seeks accounts from those involved and examines evidence before consequential decisions whenever reasonably possible, preferably privately. For a credible immediate safety threat, it may impose a temporary protective measure first, then review the case. Random chance never determines guilt or punishment.

**Still open:** exact evidence thresholds, conflicting testimony, unverified allegations, warning prerequisites, appeals reviewer, reinstatement rules and urgent-review timing. These decisions describe approved governance, not a live automated enforcement implementation.


## Confirmed procedures — questions 69–71

- **Q69 — Conflicting testimony:** Eldgrim must weigh all accounts impartially and examine independent evidence where possible. Higher standing does not confer greater credibility. If guilt cannot be reliably established, no punishment may be imposed based on speculation. Neutral, proportionate protective steps remain possible where necessary.
- **Q70 — Distinct measures:** **Warning:** identifies misconduct and requests correction. **Demotion:** removes social recognition or entrusted responsibilities. **Timeout:** temporarily restricts Discord participation. **Kick:** removes the person from the server without barring rejoining. **Ban:** prevents rejoining unless reversed. All measures require documented reasons.
- **Q71 — Prior warnings:** Minor and moderate violations ordinarily receive a warning and chance to improve. Serious or critical violations may justify stronger action without a warning. Repeated misconduct may lead to escalation, always subject to proportionality.

**Still open:** independent evidence thresholds, handling single unverified accusations, permanent-ban criteria, formal appeal review, reinstatement, and off-cycle emergency response. These are policy decisions, not proof of automated enforcement.


## Confirmed procedures — questions 72–74

- **Q72 — Permanent removal:** A ban is reserved for severe abuse, credible safety threats, deliberate exposure of private information, serious exploitation, or repeated harmful misconduct despite prior intervention. It requires reliable evidence, documented reasons and access to an appeal mechanism. Inactivity, disagreement or low internal standing alone cannot justify a ban.
- **Q73 — Emergency intervention:** Credible urgent threats to members, privacy or community safety permit immediate, proportionate restrictions or removal without advance warning or hearing. Such actions must be recorded and reviewed afterward; ordinary disputes do not qualify as emergencies.
- **Q74 — Offline protection:** The always-online bot may execute only explicitly authorized, predefined emergency protections while the local AI is inactive; designated human custodians may intervene. Complex judgments await the next agent session. Every emergency action must be logged and reviewed. **No continuous AI monitoring or completed automated enforcement is implied.**

**Open details:** precise ban-appeal process, evidence standards, emergency safeguards and implementation verification.


## Confirmed procedures — questions 75–77

- **Q75 — Individual responsibility:** In incidents involving several members, evaluate each person's actions separately. Never impose collective sanctions. Distinguish aggressors, participants, witnesses and people defending themselves; assess evidence, intent and responsibility consistently.
- **Q76 — Mistakes, jokes and deliberate harm:** Consider context, intent, impact, prior warnings and whether the person responds constructively to an explained boundary. Honest mistakes ordinarily call for clarification. Harmful jokes may warrant correction; deliberate or repeated harm may warrant stronger sanctions. Describing harassment as a joke does not excuse it.
- **Q77 — Randomness prohibited in discipline:** Chance may be used in storytelling and harmless recreation but never to determine guilt, severity, punishment, appeals or member rights. Decisions must follow evidence, established rules and proportionality.

**Still open:** precise evidence thresholds, appeal reviewer and process, reinstatement and retention. These are approved rules, not evidence of live AI moderation.


## Confirmed appeals and corrections — questions 78–80

- **Q78 — Appeals:** Members may privately challenge any consequential disciplinary action, including warnings, demotions, timeouts, kicks and bans, even after leaving the server. They receive the rule-based reason and can provide evidence without retaliation. Do not disclose Eldgrim's internal standing or inferred personalization profile.
- **Q79 — Reviewer:** Eldgrim may reconsider its decision first. Members may seek review by the founder, whose appeal decision Eldgrim cannot override. If the founder was personally involved in the underlying dispute, a designated impartial human reviewer handles the appeal.
- **Q80 — Remedy:** Promptly reverse or reduce incorrect sanctions, restore roles/privileges where technically feasible, correct records, and explain the correction to the affected member. Retain a private audit trail of the mistake; do not publish sensitive case details.

**Open operational details:** appeal submission channel, review deadlines, reviewer designation, evidence thresholds and retention. No AI moderation implementation is claimed.


## Confirmed reinstatement — question 81

A permanently banned member may return only through a successful appeal or a documented founder/impartial-reviewer decision after the original risk has been addressed. Rejoining is not automatic. The mechanism to apply reinstatement is not yet specified or implemented as AI governance.
