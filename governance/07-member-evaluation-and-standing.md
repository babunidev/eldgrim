# Chapter 07 — Member evaluation and standing

> **Status:** Profile categories and scale agreed; scoring method open  
> **Source:** Consolidated guild governance framework, 10 November 2026, v0.1. Working material, not a ratified constitution.

Four categories and 0–100 scale agreed · Evaluation formula open

Eldgrim's memory model distinguishes a Discord member from their registered in-game characters.

Each member has four intended profile categories.

| Category                | Contents                                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A — Character           | Characters, race, class, professions, level, progression, equipment, and relevant gameplay details |
| B — Social              | Interests, humor, preferences, social connections, and ways of interacting                         |
| C — Guild participation | Shared adventures, gatherings, quests, helpfulness, and contributions                              |
| D — Personal history    | Achievements, important experiences, relationships, rewards, and narrative developments            |

Persistent character sheets are intended to contain concise interpreted keywords or structured descriptors, rather than copied conversations.

The earlier design also established an internal standing score:

Internal standing

Conceptual scale

0 — Disengaged

The colors illustrate the conceptual range, not approved scoring thresholds.

Eldgrim would evaluate members during its daily cycle, recognize the strongest positive contribution, and consider whether problematic behavior warrants a negative consequence.

The scoring system is not yet safe to use for autonomous disciplinary decisions. Its weights, evidence requirements, decay, corrections, and connection to guild ranks are undefined.

Most importantly, the earlier interpretation of zero as disengagement conflicts with the guild's promise that participation is voluntary. Being absent, quiet, or uninterested in events must be distinguished from misconduct.

## Confirmed standing rules — questions 53–62

- **Q53:** The internal **0–100** score measures evidence of positive contribution (kindness, helpfulness, reliability, creativity and respectful conduct), not personal worth, popularity, attendance or character progression.
- **Q54–55:** Every *new* member starts at **50**, a neutral baseline. Existing members retain their previously established standing when the new system is introduced.
- **Q56:** Standing may rise for kindness, helping members and newcomers, creativity, accepted quest achievements, constructive dispute resolution and meaningful support of the fellowship. Simply being online or attending an event does not automatically earn points.
- **Q57:** Standing may fall for **verified** misconduct, harassment, dishonesty, deliberate disruption, abuse of entrusted responsibilities or repeated established-rule violations. Disagreement, inactivity, missed events and refusing optional activities never lower standing.
- **Q58:** Minor acts: **1–3 points**; significant acts: **4–10 points**; exceptional acts: **up to 20 points**. Adjustments must be proportional to verified context and bounded to 0–100.
- **Q59:** Profile A (character) and D (personal history) supply context without direct score changes. B (social contribution) and C (helpfulness/participation) supply evidence. Rule violations are evaluated separately. Personality preferences, playstyle and character choices cannot themselves determine standing.
- **Q60–61:** Inactivity is **neutral without time limit**. Silence, absence, missed messages and optional quests left incomplete are not misconduct. Only documented actions, never absence of action, may warrant negative adjustments. Members may resume participation without penalty or pressure.
- **Q62:** Unverified interpretations and rumors cannot reduce standing. Negative adjustments require reliable evidence; ambiguous jokes, statements and unsupported AI inferences do not justify penalties. Positive recognition requires reasonably supported observations.

**Not yet settled:** formal source reliability criteria; correction/appeal workflow; handling of the existing-members baseline when no score has been stored; relationship between a score and specific promotions or moderation. A score must not be treated as independently sufficient proof of misconduct. The earlier description of 0 as "disengaged" is superseded: inactivity is neutral, not a negative score state.


## Interview update — questions 63–64

- **Q63 — Established:** Standing may inform promotion and rewards, but it is **never sufficient on its own** to justify discipline. Sanctions require an independently established violation of existing guild rules.
- **Q64 — Open, revision requested:** The creative did **not** accept the proposed private standing review and founder-escalation procedure. The replacement mechanism is undecided; do not describe the original proposal as policy.
